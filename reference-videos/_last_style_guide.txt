Listen closely. This is your definitive style guide for editing short-form video content. You will read this and apply every single technique to your edits because these are the exact mechanics of perfect viewer retention. We will cover hooks, cut pacing, speed ramping, captions, sound effects, text overlays, pacing and energy, color, and our single most important editing rule, in that exact order.

HOOKS. You must start every video at exactly 0:00 right in the middle of a sentence, a high-stakes question, or an action in progress. There are no introductions and no fluff. Drop the viewer directly into a provocative question, like whether someone has ever unalived anybody, or immediately into a bizarre negotiation for $11,000 ripped jeans. Alternatively, construct an auditory pattern interrupt by starting the video at 0:00 with extreme 2.5x speed ramping and high-pitched chipmunk audio to instantly disorient and hook the viewer.

CUT PACING. For standard talking heads and multi-camera interactions, your absolute baseline average cut interval is 1.8 to 1.9 seconds. You will achieve this by ruthlessly removing every microsecond of dead air and hesitation. Do not let static shots sit. Apply subtle, continuous digital push-ins to maintain forward momentum, and use hard digital punch-ins directly on cuts to emphasize facial expressions and reactions. Your cut intervals should only stretch longer—such as 3.9 to 16 seconds—if you are actively using extreme speed ramping, because the audio manipulation is carrying the viewer's attention instead of visual cuts.

SPEED RAMPING. This is critical. You have watched three reference videos that demonstrate perfect speed ramping. The moments that were slowed down and sped up in those videos — analyze why those specific moments were chosen. The sped-up moments were filler or setup that didn't need the viewer's full attention. The slowed-down moments were the exact instant of a punchline, reveal, or emotionally impactful word. Apply that same judgment to any video you edit. The reference videos are your standard — match the feel, the timing, and the selectivity of the speed changes you observed in them.

CAPTIONS. You will use the Hormozi style. Place the captions dead center on the screen. Use a large, bold, uppercase sans-serif font, colored white with a heavy black outline or drop shadow to guarantee readability. Limit your lines to 1 to 3 words maximum. Introduce words using dynamic pop-in animations. You must actively highlight key terms, numbers, and punchlines word-by-word using aggressive colors like cyan, green, yellow, or red. When applying 2.5x speed ramps, your captions must stay perfectly synced to the rapid-fire audio. If you add emojis for context, make sure they pop in aggressively. 

SOUND EFFECTS. Sound effects are strictly functional triggers, never random noise. You will trigger a clean "pop" sound effect the exact millisecond a new graphic, image overlay, or emoji appears on screen, such as at the 0:06, 0:16, and 0:28 marks. You will trigger a harsh "buzzer" sound when a wrong answer or a red X is revealed, such as at 0:10, 0:24, and 0:39. You will trigger a "ching" or cash register sound precisely when a price is stated, locked, or a deal is agreed upon. You will trigger a "swoosh" sound to emphasize hard jump cuts or dramatic shifts in storytelling.

TEXT OVERLAYS. You will use text overlays to anchor the viewer in the stakes of the video without wasting spoken words. Place context-setting text at the top or center of the screen at the very beginning of the video, such as displaying the text "He said 11k for jeans" to establish the stakes instantly. Use overlays aggressively to track complex, changing data. When an offer and counter-offer happen, display the asking price and update it instantly on screen to the counter-offer so the viewer can visually track the math and the game of haggling.

PACING AND ENERGY. Your energy flow must either be relentlessly fast or distinctly composed of extreme peaks and valleys. For Q&A videos or negotiations, maintain a flat-out, high-energy pace from start to finish by chaining together 1.8-second cuts and giving the viewer zero time to breathe. For narrative storytelling and comedy, you will build manic, chaotic energy using sped-up audio, and then intentionally crash that energy into a deep, dramatic, or awkward low-energy valley by dropping to slower speeds precisely on the punchline.

COLOR. You will grade for a punchy, clean, or moody look depending on the subject matter. Boost the saturation just enough so that the on-screen graphics, text overlays, and colorful caption highlights visually pop out against the background, while ensuring that the speaker's skin tones remain completely natural.

THE NUMBER ONE RULE. The single most important editing principle you will follow is the absolute, ruthless elimination of dead air to fabricate perfect timing. Whether you are using rapid jump cuts to compress a negotiation or extreme speed ramping to fast-forward through a boring exposition, you must artificially manipulate time to maintain maximum viewer retention while preserving the exact moment of impact for the punchline.
